Field notes
How we found thirteen million dirhams inside the electricity bill.

A company signs thousands of contracts over twenty five years and keeps a register of everything it owns. The assets that need electricity to run, the cold rooms, the refrigerated trailers, the charging yard, live in three places: the supplier contracts, the asset register and the power bills. Nobody reads the three side by side. A machine does, in an afternoon, and lines them up on one table.

The biggest number is almost never hidden. The tariff written into the contracts years ago no longer matched what the assets actually drew, and the difference had been paid every month since, by an accounts team that could only see one page at a time. Thirteen point four million dirhams, one off, confirmed from the company's own records. Not an estimate. A line item.

The thesis
The score we run by: how much of your work runs on agents.

Every company sits somewhere on one line. At the left, people do everything by hand. In the middle, software helps but a person still pushes every step. At the right, agents carry the repeated work and people make the calls.

The cockpit measures where you are, workflow by workflow, and adds it up into one number. The number moves every month, because every month more of the work runs on its own. That is the only progress report a chairman needs.

The future
Humans and agents: the org chart of the next decade.

The next company has both on the chart, side by side. People set the goals, hold the budget and own the result. Agents carry the repetition: the collecting, the reconciling, the chasing, the filing.

Every agent has a named manager, a job description, a cost, and a line it never crosses. Money out, prices to customers and anything legal always stop at a person. Someone runs the agents the way a good manager runs a team.

Playbook
Build or buy: when we automate and when we source.

Once the cockpit shows where the money leaks, the next question is how to close it. If the answer is your edge, the thing your company knows better than anyone, we build it inside your walls.

If proven technology already exists somewhere in the world, we source it and bring it in, because that beats building it twice. And sometimes the honest answer is neither: the leak is a decision, not a tool, and it goes to the person who owns it.
